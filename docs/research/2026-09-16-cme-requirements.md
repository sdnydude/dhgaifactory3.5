# What a production CME grant package must satisfy: accreditor and supporter requirements

Date: 2026-09-16. Sources are primary documents (ACCME, Joint Accreditation, AMA, CMS, supporter grant portals, PhRMA Code), URLs in the appendix. Two claims were re-verified in the main session against the documents themselves: the ACCME Urgent Alert of April 14, 2026 (suspension language) and the ACCME AI guidance 1098_20260130 (named reviewers, traceability, tool disclosure, documented bias screening).

## The finding that shapes the whole build

On April 14, 2026 ACCME issued an Urgent Alert on AI in accredited CE: inadequately governed AI-generated content "can place a provider's accreditation at risk," and ACCME "reserves the right to take immediate action, including suspension of accreditation." Its January 2026 guidance (1098_20260130, with a parallel Joint Accreditation document) requires that fixed AI-generated outputs be "reviewed and approved by named, qualified individuals before dissemination," "checked for factual errors or AI hallucinations," "screened for bias," and "accompanied by version control and traceability, indicating who reviewed what, and when." Providers should "disclose the name, version, and date of use of the AI tool" and its purpose, "screen AI outputs for commercial bias" and "document the steps taken." It names "automating analysis or summaries that bypass bias, independence, or accuracy checks" as a high-risk use, and requires that PHI and PII not enter AI tools that do not meet privacy requirements, that outcomes and learner data be de-identified except for internal use, and that AI-assisted content be stored only in secure, access-controlled databases.

Consequence: provenance (which model, which version, which prompt, which human approved, when) is a first-class product requirement of the rebuild, not an observability feature. The old pipeline's default of skipping human review is, under this guidance, an accreditation risk.

## 1. ACCME requirements the documents must reflect

The Standards for Integrity and Independence in Accredited Continuing Education (effective January 1, 2022, adopted by eight accreditors) govern. Standard 1: all patient-care recommendations based on current science, evidence and clinical reasoning with a fair and balanced view; all cited research conforming to accepted standards of design, collection, analysis and interpretation; lower-evidence topics clearly identified. Standard 3: collect all financial relationships with ineligible companies from the prior 24 months, no dollar threshold; exclude owners and employees of ineligible companies (three narrow exceptions); mitigate before the person assumes a role; document the mitigation; disclose to learners before they engage, with names, companies and nature, company names only, no logos. Standard 4: a written commercial-support agreement executed before the education, signed by the accredited provider, itemizing use of funds.

The accreditation criteria are now named, not numbered (requirements document updated April 27, 2026): Educational Needs, Designed to Change, Appropriate Formats, Competencies, Analyzes Change, plus Mission, Program Analysis and Program Improvements. A professional practice gap is "the difference between health care processes or outcomes observed in practice, and those potentially achievable on the basis of current professional knowledge"; the educational need is the knowledge, competence or performance deficit underlying that gap, tied to the provider's own learners. ACCME does not mandate Moore's framework; Analyzes Change requires change in competence, performance or patient outcomes at the program level. Provisional accreditation now requires all core criteria.

## 2. What commercial supporters require

GSK publishes a five-part rubric (compliance, disease-area alignment, needs assessment and gaps, objectives and educational design, outcomes) and states that initiatives measuring competence and higher, Moore levels 4 to 7, are funding priorities. Pfizer's 2026 request fixes named sections (goals and objectives, assessment of need with quantitative baseline, target audience with sample size, project design, innovation, evaluation and outcomes, dissemination, timeline, organization detail, line-item budget), caps overhead at 28 percent, and requires a stated control or baseline strategy. Merck requires an educational needs assessment, objectives per activity, and an explicit outcomes level per activity, with 60-day US lead time and no exceptions. Takeda fixes the intended outcomes level at Moore level 4 and requires full Standards compliance.

Published rejection reasons: late submission, activity already occurred, resubmission of a declined activity, brand or generic drug names in objectives or agenda, naming faculty or planning-committee members, off-priority therapeutic area, ineligible expenses, no educational focus, and failure to identify, mitigate and disclose all conflicts. Standing terms: the applicant must be accredited; faculty names omitted; outcomes level declared; no learner food, travel, lodging or registration from grant funds; competitive review with no feedback on declines; a final outcomes report and financial reconciliation with return of unused funds; scope changes need pre-approval. The PhRMA Code (effective January 1, 2022) requires companies to use objective criteria for grant decisions and give no guidance to providers, which is why these rubrics are public.

## 3. Evidence and citation standards

ACCME publishes no list of acceptable evidence types, no citation format and no recency threshold for needs-assessment sources. The published bars are Standard 1.2's design-quality requirement and one hard recency rule: enduring materials must be reviewed at least every three years, with the review date printed. Supporters are more concrete: Merck names prior-activity learner responses, learner questions, surveys, quality metrics paired with EHR or claims performance data, published literature, published disparities of care, expert opinion and patient perspectives; GSK and Pfizer require cited quantitative baseline data. ACCME's AI guidance cites the AMA Manual of Style, 11th edition, which is the de facto convention but is not imposed by any accreditor. DHG will set and defend its own evidence, citation and recency standards; nothing external can be cited for them.

## 4. Outcomes and performance-improvement CME

Moore, Green and Gallis (2009): level 1 participation, 2 satisfaction, 3A declarative knowledge, 3B procedural knowledge, 4 competence, 5 performance, 6 patient health, 7 community health, each with named objective and subjective data sources. ACCME's October 2024 compliance guidance: satisfaction data is not sufficient; competence is not merely increased knowledge; self-reported change is acceptable when learners give examples of changes made or planned; analysis must roll up across activities against program goals and mission, and the most common noncompliance is never doing the program-level analysis. PI-CME under AMA PRA: stage A assesses current practice against performance measures by chart review, stage B implements interventions, stage C reassesses with the same measures; 5, 10 or 20 credits for A, A plus B, A plus B plus C; measures drawn from NCQA, AHRQ, CMS Quality Payment Program or the Joint Commission; the provider validates depth of participation.

## 5. Data handling

HIPAA applies to covered entities and their business associates. A standalone medical-education company producing a needs assessment from published literature, guidelines and aggregate registry or claims statistics handles no PHI. It flips in two places: a CME department inside a hospital or health system, and PI-CME stage A and C chart review or any Moore level 5 or 6 measurement touching charts, EHR or claims, which must run under the host institution's HIPAA authority. Every provider must attest that reaccreditation materials will not include individually identifiable health information. Standard 2.3 forbids sharing learner names or contact information with any ineligible company without the learner's explicit consent. Retention: attendance records six years, activity files for the accreditation term or the last twelve months, whichever is longer. No supporter publishes de-identification terms; any such term sits in the grant agreement or letter of agreement.

## 6. Published rubrics usable as automated evals

Four exist. ACCME's content-validity peer-review template: five binary questions keyed to Standards 1.1 to 1.4. The CE Educator's Toolkit worksheet guide (ACCME with SACME): a current-state versus ideal-state gap frame, six scored guiding questions (prevalence of the need, number of independent assessment sources, impact on patient care, likelihood that CE changes behavior, resource sufficiency, learner receptivity), and a Target-Action-Context-Time structure for objectives over the Anderson-Krathwohl taxonomy. The Menu of Criteria for Accreditation with Commendation: each criterion with rationale, critical elements joined by AND, and a numeric standard scaled by program size. GSK's five-part rubric. No accreditor or association publishes a scored rubric for needs assessments as such; DHG's eval suite composes one from these four.

## Requirements checklist for the rebuild (25 testable items)

- [ ] Gap statement expressed as current state versus achievable state, matching ACCME's definition.
- [ ] Educational need typed as knowledge, competence or performance and linked to the stated gap.
- [ ] Needs assessment scoped to the provider's own learners, not the general literature.
- [ ] Every gap claim carries a citation; uncited assertions fail.
- [ ] Needs assessment draws on at least two independent source types.
- [ ] Each citation's evidence type is labeled so design-quality review is possible.
- [ ] Enduring-material content carries a review date and fails validation past three years.
- [ ] Learning objectives measurable, structured Target-Action-Context-Time, derived from the stated need.
- [ ] Objectives contain no brand or generic drug names.
- [ ] Grant request contains no faculty, chair or planning-committee names.
- [ ] Outcomes plan declares a Moore level per activity and names the data source for that level.
- [ ] Outcomes plan reaches Moore level 4 or higher by default; levels 1 and 2 alone are flagged non-competitive.
- [ ] Satisfaction-only or knowledge-only evaluation fails the outcomes check.
- [ ] Program-level roll-up analysis is a distinct artifact.
- [ ] Pre/post design names a comparison baseline or control strategy.
- [ ] PI-CME output emits three stages using the same measures in A and C, with measure provenance.
- [ ] Disclosure collection covers a 24-month lookback, all amounts, all planners and faculty.
- [ ] Owners and employees of ineligible companies blocked from planner and faculty roles unless an exception is asserted.
- [ ] Mitigation steps recorded per individual, dated before role assignment.
- [ ] Learner-facing disclosure renders names, companies, nature and the mitigation statement, no logos or trade names.
- [ ] Commercial-support agreement generated with an execution date before activity start and itemized fund use.
- [ ] Budget excludes learner food, travel, lodging and registration and respects a stated overhead cap.
- [ ] Every AI-generated artifact records tool name, version, date of use, purpose, and named human reviewer with timestamp.
- [ ] Every AI-generated artifact passes a hallucination and citation-verification check and a commercial-bias screen, both logged.
- [ ] No PHI or learner PII enters any AI tool or submitted file; outputs and outcomes data de-identified by default.
